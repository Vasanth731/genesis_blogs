Chapter 3 : A Fisherman and A Pharisee - The Two Pillars of Church

What more proof do we need than this, for understanding that it's not man's status but God's calling. Peter was a simple fisherman from Galilee. He was neither wealthy nor highly educated, yet Jesus saw something far greater than what the world could see. Christ called Peter to leave behind fishing for fish and become a "fisher of men." Later, Jesus declared, "You are Peter, and on this rock I will build my Church." Though Peter stumbled, denied Christ three times, and often acted impulsively, God's grace transformed him into a fearless leader. On the day of Pentecost, Peter boldly proclaimed the Gospel, and through the power of the Holy Spirit, about 3,000 people were converted and added to the Church in a single day.

Paul, on the other hand, began his life as Saul, a highly educated Pharisee and a Roman citizen. Zealous for the Jewish law, he actively persecuted Christians, believing he was serving God. Everything changed on the road to Damascus when the risen Christ appeared to him. Blinded by the heavenly light yet enlightened by the truth, Saul repented and became Paul, dedicating his life to proclaiming Christ across nations, taking part in the missionary dimension of the church through his missionary journeys.

A fisherman and a Pharisee. One was ordinary, the other highly accomplished. One walked with Jesus during His earthly ministry, while the other encountered the risen Christ after His resurrection. Yet God used both in extraordinary ways. Their lives remind us that God's kingdom is not built on human achievements but on hearts that respond to His call. Peter teaches us that our failures do not disqualify us from God's purpose, while Paul reminds us that no past is too dark for God's grace to redeem. Together, they stand as living witnesses of God's calling.
