Chapter 1 : Multiple Timelines, Books & People, But One Storyline

When we think about the biblical canon, we realize that it is not simply a collection of books arranged at random. From Genesis to Revelation, it tells one unified story. It is remarkable that, although it was written by many authors over the course of centuries, it contains countless references to both past events and future ones through prophecy. This remarkable unity across different authors, cultures, and time periods points to a single divine source of inspiration, calling us to believe that Scripture was written under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit.

The story begins with the beautiful account of creation and the unbroken relationship between the first humans and God. However, through their disobedience, sin entered the world, and with sin came death [Romans 5:12]. Yet God's mercy did not end there. Instead, this marked the beginning of His eternal plan of redemption. Even in the midst of humanity's fall, God promised the coming of a Savior [Genesis 3:15]. As generations passed, the wickedness of humanity continued to increase. Every inclination of the human heart became consumed by evil, and God saw the depth of mankind's corruption [Genesis 6:5]. Yet Noah found favor in the eyes of God, and through him God preserved the human race from destruction. After the flood, the descendants of Noah's sons—Shem, Ham, and Japheth—spread across the earth, giving rise to the nations. As God's plan of redemption unfolded, He chose Abraham, the son of Terah, through whom He would bring salvation to the world. The covenant was then passed on to Abraham's son Isaac and later to Isaac's son Jacob, whom God renamed Israel. [Genesis 32:28]

This brings us to the story of Joseph, one of Jacob's youngest sons. Out of jealousy, Joseph's brothers betrayed him and sold him into slavery in Egypt. Yet God turned their evil intentions into good. Joseph was raised to become second in command under Pharaoh, and through the wisdom God gave him, he preserved Egypt during a severe famine. In doing so, he also saved his own family, bringing his brothers and father to Egypt, where they found refuge during the years of famine. After Joseph's death, however, the Israelites eventually became enslaved in Egypt. After about 400 years, God raised up Moses to lead His people out of bondage and toward the Promised Land. Following Moses came the period of the Judges, and later the era of the kings and the prophets. Under King Solomon, Israel experienced a time of peace, and the first Temple was built in Jerusalem, where the glory of God filled His dwelling place. Yet the people repeatedly turned away from God, and as a consequence, God allowed them to be captivated by the surrounding nations.

Throughout Israel's history, the same pattern repeated itself. Humanity continually fell into sin and proved incapable of living according to God's perfect standard. This brings us back to the prophecies of Isaiah, Jeremiah, and Daniel, which pointed to the coming Messiah—the promised Redeemer who would be rejected by His own people and offered as the sacrificial Lamb for the sins of the world. [Isaiah 53:7]. In the fullness of time, those prophecies were fulfilled. Jesus Christ was born, lived a perfect and sinless life, fulfilled the Law, bore the sins of all humanity upon Himself, and was crucified on the cross. Through His death, He conquered sin, and through His resurrection, He defeated death, opening the way for all who believe in Him to receive eternal life. [John 3:16]

Before ascending into heaven, Christ entrusted His followers with the Great Commission—to go into all the world and proclaim the good news of salvation. [Matthew 28:19-20]. Empowered by the Holy Spirit, the apostles went throughout the known world preaching the Gospel, making disciples, and planting churches. Over time, several major centers of Christianity emerged, including Jerusalem, Antioch, Alexandria, Rome, and Constantinople. From these centers, the Gospel continued to spread to the ends of the earth through missionary work. The Church has continued this mission through the successors of the apostles, guided by the Holy Spirit, faithfully proclaiming the Gospel from generation to generation until the end of the age, awaiting the glorious second coming of our Lord Jesus Christ.
